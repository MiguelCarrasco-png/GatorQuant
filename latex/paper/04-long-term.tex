\section{Long-term operation}\label{sec:long}

No rule in \S1 names a date, a route or a planet. Rules refer to $T_0$, $R_e$, $R_h$ and closures, which every Branch
computes from the published common geometry at the moment it acts. That is why the same rules ran unchanged at +1,
+10, +93.1 and +100 years (appendix E5). What rests on rules and what rests on the model is marked as in \S2.

\subsection{Rules that keep working}

\textbf{A long period with no usable route, and restoration} (rules). Locks do not expire (R6), so nothing financial
happens during the gap: every obligation keeps its funded owner and its lock. On the model a settlement is never cut
off geometrically (E4: a route of at most three links always exists), and R16 moves a session off a pinned route that
closes, so a gap needs an incident or a failure beyond the model. On restoration each lock holder resubmits (R8) on a
fresh session (R15). The decider's answer is already recorded (R7), so a resubmission arriving after any delay gets
the same answer, and SETTLE carries the payoff fixed at the print, not at arrival.

\textbf{A backlog after a route reopens} (rules, with stated capacity). Only lock holders have anything to send, and
each sends at most one copy per open deal: an endpoint retry discards its earlier unlaunched copy, and a
resubmission replaces the old one. Records to one peer share packets (14 per packet). A Branch's 10-packet recovery
reserve alone carries 140 resubmitted deals per 24 h, and the reserve cannot be eaten by new trade (R14). The link
capacity (1 packet/s, 10{,}000-packet queue) and the 64-packet window per peer allow 896 deal records in flight per
peer, far above any backlog our demand rate can create.

\textbf{An obligation that matures after our longest example} (rules). Maturity processing does not depend on how long
a position has been open: the referee sees the settling print locally, records the payoff, sends SETTLE once, and the
pledge holder pulls (R8) at print $+\,R_e$. A session is not state: if it expired during a long contract the pledge
holder simply handshakes a new one before pulling. A contract therefore needs no traffic between opening and maturity
except the information-only print forwards, which also keep its session alive.

\textbf{A transport packet expires while a commitment is unresolved} (rules). Expiry stops forwarding; it never ends
a lock or cancels a decision. The deal is identified by its deal ID, not its packet ID. A resubmission is a new packet
with the same deal ID, and if the old copy arrives later it is a duplicate LOCK, answered from the record (R7).

\subsection{Continuing without a reset}

\textbf{Order and contract lifecycles} \implemented. Offer: posted $\to$ filled, withdrawn or expired. Deal: locked
$\to$ committed or declined $\to$ released. Position: open $\to$ discharged at the settling print $\to$ pledge ended by
SETTLE. Every state is on a durable ledger; none needs a reset. A new contract series (new contract ID, entry = the
print at listing, next settling print 288 h later) is listed by the referee after each maturity \extension.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[R18] \emph{Declared demand and load above it} \implemented, not reached in tests. Each Branch accepts
\textbf{700 cross-planet deal records per rolling 24 h} (LOCKs it sends plus COMMIT, DECLINE and SETTLE replies), the
capacity of its 56-packet trade allowance at 14 records per packet, less 10\% headroom. Below 40 originated packets in
the window a record goes out at once. Above 40 the Branch batches: at most one packet per peer per hour, carrying every
waiting record. Within the allowance, replies and SETTLE go first (they end other Branches' locks), then
resubmissions, then new LOCKs. When the allowance is full, new cross-planet acceptances wait in a local FIFO queue
\emph{without} a lock, and an acceptance that cannot leave within its offer's life (R5) is refused locally. Local
deals (R3) are never limited. Load above 700 records therefore costs time and refusals, never an unfunded lock.
\item[R19] \emph{Bounded storage and identifiers} \implemented. Deal counters, session counters and contract IDs are
8 bytes and never reused. At the full system quota (600 packets $\times$ 14 records per day) an 8-byte counter lasts
$6 \times 10^{12}$ years, far beyond the 200-year horizon we design and test for (E4). If a counter ever reached its limit, its Branch would
refuse new deals and sessions locally and still settle every open one. A decider deletes its decision record for a
deal once the initiator's low-water mark (batch header) passes it; packet IDs are kept for lifetime + 30 days, as the
brief requires. The ledger journal itself grows by 64 bytes per record, at most 0.54 MB per day for the whole system,
kept in archival storage (assumed infrastructure).
\item[R20] \emph{Capital replenishment and fees} \implemented, schedule \$0 in every run. No institution needs
capital: Branches never take a position, extend credit or guarantee anything; every guarantee is backed by a client's
own lock. Each Branch may charge a fixed fee per cross-planet deal it initiates, published in advance, debited from
the initiating client's free balance in the same posting as the lock and credited to the Branch's own account on its
home ledger. Fee income pays operating costs and never backs a client obligation. Clients replenish capital only by
trading gains and by contributions from other accounts, which are ordinary transfers under R6--R9. Nothing is
endowed.
\item[R21] \emph{No automatic clearing.} No obligation is ever cleared by elapsed time, a window closing or packet
expiry. Each one keeps its funded owner (the lock holder) until a recorded COMMIT, DECLINE or SETTLE ends it.
\end{description}

\textbf{Quota enforcement on both services.} Backbone: each Branch counts its own originations in a sliding 24 h log
and never exceeds 66 (R14), so the system never exceeds 594 of 600 without any Branch seeing another's count. Direct:
no rule uses it; a client's own gateway enforces 12 packets per 24 h and 60 s spacing on anything it sends.

\textbf{Infrastructure we assume.} A durable ledger and journal at each Branch, at its gateway; a TDB clock at each
Branch, re-synchronised so its error stays well below 1 s (\S\ref{sec:deploy}); the common ephemeris, from which
each Branch computes closures, $T_0$ and foresight routes; archival storage. Nothing else: no hub, no shared database,
no trusted third party.
