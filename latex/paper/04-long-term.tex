\section{Long-term operation}\label{sec:long}

No rule in \S1 names a date, a route or a planet. Rules refer to $T_0$, $R_e$, $R_h$ and closures, which every Branch computes from the
published common geometry when it acts, so the same rules ran unchanged at +1, +10, +93.1 and +100 years (appendix E5).

\subsection{Rules that keep working}

\textbf{No usable route, then restoration} (rules). Locks do not expire (R6), so nothing financial happens during the gap: every obligation
keeps its funded owner and lock. On the model a settlement is never cut off geometrically (E4: a route of at most three links always exists)
and R16 leaves a pinned route that closes, so a gap needs an incident beyond the model. On restoration each lock holder resubmits (R8) on a
fresh session (R15); the decider's answer is already recorded (R7), and SETTLE carries the payoff fixed at the print, not at arrival.

\textbf{A backlog after a route reopens} (rules, stated capacity). Only lock holders send, at most one copy per open deal (a newer copy
replaces the older, R8), 14 records per packet. A Branch's 10-packet reserve carries 140 resubmitted deals per 24 h and new trade cannot eat it
(R14); the link (1 packet/s, 10{,}000-packet queue) and the 64-packet window allow 896 records in flight per peer.

\textbf{An obligation that matures after our longest example} (rules). Maturity does not depend on how long a position has been open: the
referee sees the settling print locally, records the payoff and sends SETTLE; the pledge holder resubmits (R8) if it has not arrived. A session is
not state: one that expired during a long contract is handshaken anew. Between opening and maturity only the information-only print forwards travel.
\textbf{A transport packet expires while a commitment is unresolved} (rules): expiry stops forwarding and never ends a lock; the deal is
identified by deal ID, so a late old copy is a duplicate LOCK answered from the record (R7).

\subsection{Continuing without a reset}

\textbf{Lifecycles} \implemented. Offer: posted $\to$ filled, withdrawn or expired. Deal: locked $\to$ committed or declined $\to$ released.
Position: open $\to$ discharged at the settling print $\to$ pledge ended by SETTLE. Every state is on a durable ledger. A new contract series
(new ID, entry = the print at listing, settling print 288 h later) is listed after each maturity \extension.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[R18] \emph{Declared demand and load above it} \implemented, exercised in S2 at reduced slices (new-LOCK budget $S-10$ packets, local queue
without a lock, R5 refusal; the 40-packet batching threshold is not simulated). Each Branch accepts \textbf{700 cross-planet deal records per
rolling 24 h}, the capacity of its 56-packet trade allowance at 14 records per packet less 10\% headroom. Above 40 originated packets in the window it
sends at most one packet per peer per hour; replies and SETTLE go first (they end other Branches' locks), then resubmissions, then new LOCKs. When the
allowance is full, new acceptances wait in a local FIFO queue \emph{without} a lock and are refused locally if they cannot leave within their offer's
life (R5). Local deals (R3) are never limited. Overload costs time and refusals, never an unfunded lock.
\item[R19] \emph{Bounded storage and identifiers} \implemented. Deal, session and contract counters are 8 bytes, never reused; at the full quota
(600 packets $\times$ 14 records a day) one lasts $6 \times 10^{12}$ years, and a Branch at its limit refuses new deals and still settles open ones. A
decider deletes a decision once the initiator's low-water mark passes it; packet IDs are kept lifetime + 30 days (brief). The journal grows at most
0.54 MB a day system-wide, in archival storage (assumed).
\item[R20] \emph{Capital replenishment and fees} \implemented, schedule \$0 in every run. Branches never take a position, extend credit or guarantee
anything; every guarantee is a client's own lock, so no institution needs capital. A Branch may charge a published fixed fee per deal it initiates,
debited from the client's free balance in the lock's posting; fees never back a client obligation. Clients replenish capital by trading gains and
ordinary transfers (R6--R9). Nothing is endowed.
\item[R21] \emph{No automatic clearing.} No obligation is cleared by elapsed time, a closing window or packet expiry; each keeps its funded owner
until a recorded COMMIT, DECLINE or SETTLE ends it.
\item[R22] \emph{Detect a Branch that breaks the rules} \extension, implemented and tested (S2). The brief assumes operators follow the rules; this adds
detection, not enforcement. Identities are unforgeable (brief). A lock holder applies a COMMIT or DECLINE only if its echoed terms equal its LOCK, applies a
SETTLE only if the amount equals the payoff \emph{it recomputes} from the settling print in the record, and logs a different second outcome for a closed
deal; a failing record is not applied and the lock stays. That the print is genuine is the Price Board's signature, which the remote Branch does not check
(assumption). Of four injected faults three are caught and, without R22, two change a balance; a Branch that never sends its decision is not caught.
\item[R23] \emph{Silent Price Board} \extension, tested. If no settling print exists 24 h after maturity, the referee voids the contract, frees its own
margin and sends SETTLE with a void flag; the remote Branch returns the pledge. Cost: both margins locked 24\,h longer (\SimVoidExtraDollarHours\ \$-hours).
\item[R24] \emph{Griefing cap} \extension, analysed only. An offer holder who withdraws after a LOCK is on its way (R7 reason 1) freezes the buyer's
money for one round trip: \$25{,}000 for \GriefVMHours\ h (\GriefVMDollarHours\ \$-hours; \GriefUranusHours\ h from Uranus; \GriefFutHours\ h for the
future's margin), measured. A Branch refuses a (client, offer holder) pair's offers for 7 days after \GriefCap\ such declines in 24 h, bounding the loss at
\GriefCap\ round trips per pair per week.
\end{description}

\textbf{Quota on both services.} Each Branch counts its own originations in a sliding 24 h log and never exceeds 66 (R14), so the system stays at or
below 594 of 600 though no Branch sees another's count. No rule uses the direct service; a client's gateway enforces 12 packets per 24 h and 60 s spacing.
\textbf{Infrastructure we assume:} a durable ledger and journal at each Branch's gateway; a TDB clock at each, error well below 1 s (\S\ref{sec:deploy}); the
common ephemeris, from which each Branch computes closures, $T_0$ and foresight routes; archival storage. No hub, shared database or trusted third party.
