\section{Architecture and operating rulebook}

\textbf{The design in one paragraph.} Every settlement has one \emph{Branch}, and every NeoDollar and share sits on
exactly one Branch's \emph{home ledger}. Nothing is ever owed across light-lag without being locked first: before a
cross-planet deal or a price contract opens, each side's full obligation is locked on its own home ledger
(\emph{money on the table}). A cross-planet deal touches exactly two ledgers: the initiator's Branch \emph{locks},
the Branch holding the offer \emph{decides once} and commits, and the initiator \emph{releases} when the commit
arrives. A price contract is refereed by the Branch where its price is published, and a remote side's margin stays
\emph{pledged at home} until the referee's \emph{Settle} arrives. No timer ever ends a lock, and only the side holding
a lock ever resends. There are no margin calls, no credit and no central hub. Rules are numbered R1--R24 and cited by
number in both documents. \implemented\ marks an exact procedure of this design, exercised in our simulator unless
noted; \extension\ marks a rule that no guarantee G1--G3 relies on (R22--R24 are implemented and tested, but sit outside
the brief's baseline of Branches that follow the published rules).

\subsection{Operating rulebook}

\textbf{Ledger rules} (all \implemented).
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[R1] \emph{One home ledger.} Only the Branch of a settlement changes balances held there. Ledgers, deal
counters, recorded decisions and packet IDs are durable; they survive crashes and resets.
\item[R2] \emph{Free balance only.} A Branch executes a client instruction only against \emph{free} balance
(balance minus reserved minus locked). Anything else is refused locally and nothing is sent. Clients cannot unlock,
reserve or move a locked asset; only the Branch record changes listed in R6--R10 can. This is what stops a client
from spending a pledged asset twice, whatever it claims to know.
\item[R3] \emph{Local deals.} A deal between two clients of one Branch is one atomic posting on one ledger, with no
network message and no quota.
\item[R4] \emph{Firm offers.} A client posts an offer at its own Branch with terms and an expiry hour; the Branch
reserves the assets until the offer fills, is withdrawn locally, or expires. A cross-planet deal always accepts a firm
offer. An offer on a price contract expires no later than the contract's freeze (R12). \emph{Advertising} \extension:
a client announces its own offer to remote clients by direct service, which is client communication under its own quota
(12 per 24 h) and creates no shared record; terms are rechecked at LOCK time (R7), so a lost bulletin means no deal and
a stale one a decline. At 27.9 AU one copy arrives with probability 11\%, twelve copies with 75\%. Backbone packets
for price discovery: none; the 28 launches per trade start at the LOCK.
\item[R5] \emph{Offer life.} An initiator Branch accepts a remote offer only if a second endpoint attempt, unless it
too is abandoned, must arrive before expiry: $\text{expiry} - t \ge R_e + T_0 + 3\sum R_h$ on its pinned route
($R_e = 2T_0 + 24$\,h, $R_h = 2\times$hop flight $+$ 1\,h, all at enqueue). Otherwise it refuses locally and locks
nothing. A decline then needs two abandoned attempts (\S\ref{sec:risks}). At h 0 this is \EtwoLifeEarthNeptune\ h
from Earth to Neptune and \EtwoLifeJupiterCeres\ h from Jupiter to Ceres.
\end{description}

\textbf{Cross-planet deal: lock, commit, release} (all \implemented). The traced value move (VM) is in appendix S1: Terra locks \$25{,}000 at h 0.0003, Neptune commits at h 4.1751, Terra releases at h 8.3499; 28 launches, 3 quota packets.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[R6] \emph{Lock.} The initiator Branch locks its client's leg and sends LOCK with the full terms and a never-reused
deal ID (initiator Branch + 8-byte counter). A lock ends only on COMMIT, DECLINE or SETTLE recorded for that deal,
never on a timer.
\item[R7] \emph{Decide once.} The Branch holding the offer decides on the \emph{first} arrival of the LOCK. It declines
only for: (1) offer filled, withdrawn or expired at arrival; (2) terms differ from the offer; (3) LOCK carries ABORT
and the deal is undecided; (4) malformed record or unknown account. Otherwise it commits. It records the decision
durably \emph{before} replying, and answers every later copy with the same recorded decision and original terms.
On COMMIT it moves the offered asset to the initiator Branch's inter-Branch account and credits its own client the
proceeds as a claim on itself, backed by the initiator's lock. That client may spend it at once.
\item[R8] \emph{Only the lock holder resends; it resubmits on contact and at the reserve's pace.} The deciding or
refereeing Branch never resends on its own, except that the referee sends SETTLE and, like transport, retries it at
the endpoint timer $R_e$. Once a lock holder has held its lock one $R_e$ (for a pledge: from the settling print)
with no recorded answer it resubmits the same LOCK, in one batch with every other probed lock to that peer, then every
$P = 2.4$\,h per packet ($24/10$: the pace its 10-packet reserve affords, stretched when more than 14 locks share the
peer). It also resubmits at once on any packet from the peer (at most once an hour), opens a fresh session if none is
up, and drops its older copies of a deal when a newer one is sent or the deal ends. The decider answers a copy with
the recorded answer, at most once an hour per deal. A resubmission is always safe: the answer is the recorded one.
The earlier timer-only version (resubmit every $R_e$) is kept as the baseline in S2.
\item[R9] \emph{Release.} On COMMIT the initiator debits its client's lock to the deciding Branch's inter-Branch
account and credits its client the bought asset as a claim backed by its own inter-Branch holding there. On DECLINE
the lock returns to the client's free balance. A trade is complete at the later of the two spendable moments.
\end{description}

\textbf{Inter-Branch claims} \implemented. A \emph{claim} is a client balance on its own Branch's ledger, backed by that
Branch's inter-Branch holding at another Branch or by a release it is owed (a recorded \emph{pending release}). It is
spendable from the moment it is credited, \emph{before} the backing has moved, because the backing is an encumbered lock
that only a recorded outcome can end: Triton's \$25{,}000 is spendable at h 4.18 while Terra's release lands at h 8.35, and
the simulator checks after every step that every claim is covered. Clients never pledge a claim themselves: only a Branch's
holdings back anything. If Terra resells her 200 Ares to a buyer at Uranus, Terra posts a firm offer (R4), Earth Branch
reserves the claim, and Uranus Branch runs R6--R9 as initiator: Uranus Branch ends holding a claim \emph{on Earth Branch},
which is backed by Earth Branch's holding at Neptune, a chain of two links. Each link is covered by an encumbered
holding at its issuer, and the per-step check applies link by link; chain depth is not limited by the rules, each extra
link adds one more light-time to redemption, and we trace one link only (\conditional\ beyond it). A Branch redeems an
inter-Branch account only by a deal under the same rules, and nothing nets automatically (R21): two Branches that hold
balances on each other net them by one explicit deal.

\textbf{Message format.} Payload $\le 960$ B, fixed width: a 16-byte batch header (sender, receiver, count, and a
\emph{low-water mark}: every deal of this sender with this receiver numbered below it is closed) plus up to 14
records of 64 B. One record layout serves LOCK, COMMIT, DECLINE and SETTLE: type, flags (ABORT, RESUBMISSION),
deal ID, offer or contract ID, both accounts, two legs (asset, quantity), time, reason or amount; a SETTLE also
carries the settling print's value in 4 of the 4 reserved bytes. Replies echo the full LOCK terms, so a mismatch is
detected rather than silently accepted (R22). Records waiting for the same peer share a
packet.

\subsection{Products}

\textbf{Ares Habitat shares (equity)} \implemented. Unit: one share; 5{,}000 exist. Location: the home ledger where
held. Traded locally (R3) or cross-planet by delivery versus payment (R6--R9) at the price in a firm offer. No
maturity, margin or valuation: each side delivers what it already holds, so neither side has exposure beyond the
price it chose. A buyer at another settlement holds a claim on its own Branch, backed by that Branch's inter-Branch
holding at the issuing ledger; it can sell it or move it on under the same rules. \textbf{Cash transfer}
\implemented\ (same records with one leg empty; not separately traced).

\textbf{Ceres Iron capped future (futures)} \implemented. Cash-settled on Ceres Iron, \$ per tonne. Price source: the
Ceres Iron Price Board, one signed print daily at h 0, 24, \ldots, 288, released locally at Ceres. Referee Branch:
Ceres. Entry \$100 (the h 0 print); multiplier 100 t (notional \$10{,}000 per contract); cap $\pm30\%$, so the
settling price is clipped to [70, 130]. The \emph{settling print} is the h 288 print only (maturity); interim prints
are information and move no money. Payoff to the long $= 25 \times 100 \times (\mathrm{clip}(P_{288}) - 100)$, the
short the opposite. No early close-out; held to maturity. Traced position: Callisto Foundry (Jupiter) long 25,
Ceres Iron Works (Ceres) short 25, notional \$250{,}000.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[R10] \emph{Pledge at home; referee settles.} The remote side's Branch locks its maximum loss on its own ledger
and sends LOCK; the referee decides it as in R7 against the local side's firm offer. \emph{Position open} = the COMMIT
record at the referee (both margins encumbered, position recorded). The remote lock is not released by COMMIT; only
SETTLE ends it. At the settling print the referee computes the clipped payoff $Y$, records it (\emph{discharge}) and
sends SETTLE once. If the short wins, the referee credits it $Y$ at once as a claim backed by the remote pledge, and
SETTLE tells the remote Branch to pay $Y$ from the pledge to the referee's inter-Branch account and return the rest.
If the long wins, the referee moves $Y$ from the short's margin to the remote Branch's inter-Branch account and SETTLE
tells the remote Branch to return the pledge and credit its client $Y$. The pull rule (R8) recovers a lost SETTLE.
\item[R11] \emph{The margin rule}, stated before either price path and identical in both: \emph{margin per side =
cap $\times$ entry $\times$ multiplier $\times$ contracts} = \$3{,}000 per contract, \$75{,}000 for 25, locked in full
before the position opens. An order is refused if free balance is below its margin. There are no margin calls, no
variation payments and no interim valuation. At maturity the loser's lock pays the winner
$100 \times 25 \times |\mathrm{clip}(P_{288}) - 100|$ and the rest of each lock is released. The rule uses only the
contract terms and positions, never a price that has not been published. Default is impossible by construction: the
largest possible payoff is already encumbered, so a loser whose Branch is cut off is a \emph{funded default} at
worst, with the winner holding a backed claim until SETTLE lands.
\item[R12] \emph{Prints and freeze.} The referee receives each print by local access. It forwards interim prints to
every Branch with an open pledge over the backbone (information only, 1 packet each); that Branch hands them to its
clients locally. From h 264 (24 h before the settling print) the referee accepts no new positions; offers on the
contract expire by then (R4).
\end{description}

\textbf{General framework} \extension. Any product whose maximum loss per side is fixed by its terms fits R10--R11 unchanged (option writer pledges the payoff, bond issuer the coupons and principal, a lender's cash moves by R6--R9 against a pledged collateral lock, a short sale is a borrow plus a sale). Unbounded-loss products (naked shorts, uncapped futures) are not offered: they would need credit across light-lag.

\subsection{Communication rules}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=sameline]
\item[R13] \emph{Classification.} LOCK, COMMIT, DECLINE, SETTLE and forwarded prints create or carry shared exchange
records, so they are official coordination and travel only on the backbone between Branches. Client instructions go
to the client's own Branch by local access. No rule uses the direct service; clients may use it for their own
observations under their own quota (12 per 24 h, 60 s apart, enforced at the sending gateway).
\item[R14] \emph{Quota slices.} No Branch can see the system-wide rolling count, so each enforces a fixed slice of
66 originated packets per rolling 24 h ($9 \times 66 = 594 \le 600$). Ten of the 66 are a \emph{recovery reserve}
usable only by resubmissions and replacement handshakes, so new trade can never stop a lock holder from resending.
\item[R15] \emph{Sessions.} A Branch handshakes the session for a planned pair 72 h ahead (room for one lost SYN on
the slowest pair before use), on the route pinned by \emph{foresight}: the fastest simple route of at most three links
that stays open from now until planned use + 24 h, not simply the fastest now. The lock holder sends a one-record
keep-alive after 120 h idle, inside the 168 h session expiry.
\item[R16] \emph{Re-route.} Before each send a Branch checks its pinned route in both directions. If a known
geometric closure would hold the packet more than 24 h at any hop, it opens a fresh session on the foresight route
(1 quota) and sends there; scheduled maintenance is waited out. It never launches on a link whose reverse direction
closes within one $R_h$, because the hop receipt could not come back (appendix E4).
\item[R17] \emph{One timescale.} Every expiry, freeze, print hour and decision time is in TDB hours from the epoch;
no ledger uses its local proper time (\S\ref{sec:deploy}).
\end{description}
