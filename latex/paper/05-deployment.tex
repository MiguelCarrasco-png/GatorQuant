\section{Deployment assessment}\label{sec:deploy}

The baseline leaves out real effects on purpose. Because every obligation is locked before it exists, an effect
that delays, loses or reorders messages can change \emph{when} a deal completes, never \emph{whether} it is funded.
The two effects that could change \emph{which} outcome settles (a deadline decided on the wrong clock, a print that
cannot arrive) are answered by rules R17 and R10/R12. ``Derived'' marks our own estimate from the cited formula with
circular, coplanar orbits: order of magnitude, not an ephemeris value.

\textbf{Where our assumptions stop.} All orbital statements are about fixed Kepler ellipses, which drift from the
real solar system over decades; our E4 and E5 results are claims about the model. We assume Branches follow their
rules; a malicious or colluding Branch could, for example, commit a deal and never record it, and handling that
(signed decisions, independent audit of inter-Branch accounts) is an \extension\ we did not design. We assume durable
storage never loses a recorded decision; if it did, G2 would fail for that deal.

\textbf{Summary.} (1) Lost packets cost time, never money: deals are all or nothing and contracts pay in full
(G1--G3, rules alone). (2) Every product is offered at all nine settlements (Table~\ref{tab:service}). (3) Over 200
years every pair keeps \EfourWorstFore\% availability (R15); the hardest epoch costs \EfiveLate\ h and the worst incident
\SimSTwoLost\ h, never a balance.
