\section{Guarantees, conditions and costs}\label{sec:guar}

Each guarantee says what holds, what it rests on (\textbf{rules}: true at any epoch whatever the network does;
\textbf{model}: shown on the common Kepler model, evidence not proof), and what participants pay for it.

\textbf{G1. Funding is conserved and no asset serves two uses} (rules). Every posting is a balanced pair on one
ledger (R1); a cross-planet deal changes two ledgers by equal and opposite inter-Branch entries, which net to zero.
A reserved or locked amount is not free balance, so no second instruction can touch it (R2), and only the recorded
outcome of its own deal ends a lock (R6). No institution starts with or creates assets. The simulator checks
conservation and single use after every ledger step: \SimChecks\ checks over \SimRuns\ runs, none failed.

\textbf{G2. A cross-planet deal is all or nothing} (rules). The deciding Branch records exactly one outcome per deal
ID before replying (R7) and repeats it to every copy; the initiator's lock persists until that outcome arrives (R6,
R8). So either both legs move (COMMIT, then release) or neither does (DECLINE). Between COMMIT and release the
bought asset already sits in the initiator Branch's inter-Branch account at the deciding ledger, and the initiator
Branch owes its client the release on arrival. Neither client ever holds an unbacked claim.

\textbf{G3. Every price contract pays in full, in both directions} (rules). Each side's largest possible loss under the
clipped payoff is locked before the position opens (R11). Whatever the settling print, the winner is paid from
encumbered assets, with no margin call and no default; the binding case (print 140, clipped to 130) pays exactly the
whole \$75{,}000 lock. If the loser's Branch is cut off at maturity the winner holds a backed claim (funded default
form) until SETTLE lands; nothing is ever unbacked.

\textbf{G4. Every lock ends, if the two Branches can eventually exchange packets} (rules + model). Only the lock holder
resends, it never gives up (R8), and its recovery reserve cannot be consumed by new trade (R14), so the only thing
that can keep a lock open is a network that never carries a packet between the two Branches. On the model, that
never happens: in 200 years every settlement can always reach every other by a route of at most three links (E4,
\EfourNeverCut), and foresight pinning keeps 100\% availability in every 24 h window. Random loss only delays: with
no incident, endpoint attempts are more than 24 h apart, share no window, and are independent, so the chance that
$k$ attempts all fail on our worst pinned route is at most $p^k$, $p = \SimWorstAttemptLoss$.

\textbf{G5. Completion times} (model, \conditional). Value move: Triton spendable at h \SimVMCommit, trade complete
at h \SimVMComplete. Future: position open at h \SimFutOpen; at maturity discharge and backed claim at h 288.0003,
spendable for the remote side at h \SimFutSpendable\ in both price directions. Under random loss (E2 Monte Carlo,
no incident) the Earth--Neptune trade completes at its no-loss time with probability \EtwoVMEarthNoLoss\%, within
24 h of it \EtwoVMEarthDay\%; the settlement is spendable at its no-loss time \EtwoSettleNoLoss\% and within 6 h
\EtwoSettleSixH\%. The fallback is always the same: retries, then resubmission (R8), with the lock in place. No
conditional time is a promise: random loss means no unconditional deadline exists.

\textbf{What participants pay or give up.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Capital.} The future locks \$150{,}000 (\SimFutUtil\% of all cash) for 288 h, \SimFutDollarHours\
\$-hours per run; capital efficiency 0.60 (peak locked / notional). A share buyer locks the full price for one round
trip (VM: \$25{,}000 for 8.35 h). No leverage: a client can open only what its free balance covers.
\item \emph{Use of money during outages.} A lock is never broken by a timer, so a client cut off from its counterparty
cannot get its money back early. In S2, Callisto's \$75{,}000 stays locked \SimSTwoLost\ h longer than planned.
\item \emph{Latency.} One light round trip plus relay time per deal: 8.2--12.4 h for Neptune at h 0, 1.4 h for
Jupiter--Ceres. Far clients see each print later than Ceres clients (\S\ref{sec:risks}).
\item \emph{Product limits.} Only bounded-loss products; futures held to maturity; no new positions in the last 24 h;
remote offers must outlive a second attempt (R5): 34--86 h at h 0.
\item \emph{Packets.} 28 backbone launches per cross-planet trade (3 count against the quota); 124 for a 288-hour
future including 11 forwarded prints (15 quota). No direct-service packets.
\end{itemize}
