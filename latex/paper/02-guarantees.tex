\section{Guarantees, conditions and costs}\label{sec:guar}

Each guarantee says what holds, what it rests on (\textbf{rules}: true at any epoch whatever the network does;
\textbf{model}: shown on the common Kepler model, evidence not proof), and what participants pay for it. We claim no formal proof. For G1--G3 the argument is
Table~\ref{tab:inv}: five invariants, the rules that can change each, and why every step preserves it. The evidence is a check of every invariant after
\emph{every} ledger step of the \SimRuns\ declared runs and of \FuzzRuns\ fault-injected runs (duplicates, reordering, contradictions, resets, a crash after every
ledger step, launch loss up to 35\%; appendix S1): \FuzzSteps\ checked steps, \FuzzFailures\ violations. The fuzzer also found three errors in our own simulator, none a
rule, all fixed (appendix S1).

\textbf{G1. Funding is conserved and no asset serves two uses} (rules; I1--I3). Every posting is a balanced pair on one ledger (R1); a cross-planet deal changes
two ledgers by equal and opposite inter-Branch entries. A reserved or locked amount is not free balance, so no second instruction can touch it (R2); only the
recorded outcome of its own deal ends a lock (R6). No institution starts with or creates assets.

\textbf{G2. A cross-planet deal is all or nothing} (rules; I4--I5). The deciding Branch records one outcome per deal ID before replying (R7) and repeats it to every
copy; the initiator's lock persists until that outcome arrives (R6, R8). Either both legs move (COMMIT, then release) or neither does (DECLINE). Between COMMIT and
release the bought asset sits in the initiator Branch's inter-Branch account, and no client ever holds an unbacked claim.

\textbf{G3. Every price contract pays in full, in both directions} (rules). Each side's largest loss under the clipped payoff is locked before the position opens
(R11), so the winner is paid from encumbered assets, with no margin call and no default; the binding case (print 140, clipped to 130) pays exactly the whole
\$75{,}000 lock. If the loser's Branch is cut off at maturity the winner holds a backed claim until SETTLE lands.

\textbf{G4. Every lock ends, within a bound, if the two Branches can exchange packets} (rules + model). Only the lock holder resends and never gives up (R8); its
reserve cannot be eaten by new trade (R14). Once a packet can cross, the lock ends at the next resubmission, at most $P\lceil n/14\rceil$ = 2.4 h later for $n$ open locks
to the peer, or on the first packet from it, plus a round trip: in S2 service resumed \SimSTwoFRSpendableAfter\ h after Ceres reconnected (h 358). The bound fails only
while a \emph{handshake} is stuck: a reset peer's SYN lost to a second incident waits one $R_e$ (25 h); the worst stacked incident ends \SimStackAfter\ h after its last
fault. A Branch gone for good is outside the baseline: its locks stay, every other claim stays covered, and no rule recovers them. On the model a settlement is never cut
off: in 200 years every one reaches every other by at most three links (E4), and foresight pinning gives 100\% availability in every 24 h window. Random loss
only delays: with no incident, endpoint attempts are over 24 h apart and independent, so $k$ attempts all fail on our worst pinned route with probability at most $p^k$,
$p = \SimWorstAttemptLoss$.

\textbf{G5. Completion times} (model, \conditional). Value move: Triton spendable at h \SimVMCommit, trade complete at h \SimVMComplete. Future: position open at
h \SimFutOpen; discharge and backed claim at h 288.0003; spendable for the remote side at h \SimFutSpendable\ in both price directions. Under random loss (E2, no
incident) the service table gives, for all nine settlements, the probability of finishing within 24 h of the no-loss time: a share trade 72.8--78.9\%, the future's settlement
92.1\% (Neptune) to $>$99.99\% (Mars to Saturn); on-time probabilities and the loss sweep are in E2. At four times the brief's loss R5 declines
\EtwoSweepLossFourDecline\% of Earth trades (it is tuned to the stated law). The fallback is always retries, then resubmission (R8), lock in place; no time here is a
promise, because random loss admits no unconditional deadline.

\textbf{What participants pay or give up.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Capital.} The future locks \$150{,}000 (\SimFutUtil\% of all cash) for 288 h, \SimFutDollarHours\ \$-hours per run; capital efficiency 0.60 (peak locked /
notional). A share buyer locks the full price for one round trip (VM: \$25{,}000 for 8.35 h). No leverage: a client opens only what its free balance covers.
\item \emph{Money during outages.} A lock is never broken by a timer. In S2-FR Callisto's \$75{,}000 pledge and \$57{,}500 payoff wait \SimSTwoFRLost\ h more
(\SimSTwoFRExtraDollarHours\ extra \$-hours); stacked, \SimStackLost\ h.
\item \emph{Latency.} One light round trip plus relay time: 8.2--12.4 h for Neptune at h 0, 1.4 h for Jupiter--Ceres. Far clients see each print later than Ceres clients
(\S\ref{sec:risks}).
\item \emph{Product limits.} Only bounded-loss products; futures held to maturity; no new positions in the last 24 h; remote offers must outlive a second attempt (R5):
34--86 h at h 0.
\item \emph{Packets.} 28 backbone launches per cross-planet trade (3 quota); \SimFutPackets\ for the 288-hour future (\SimFutQuota\ quota). Interim prints move no money, so R12 does not forward them; forwarding all 11 (variant FR-full) costs \SimFullLaunches\ launches (\SimFullQuota\ quota) with identical times and balances and gives remote clients prices before maturity.
\end{itemize}
