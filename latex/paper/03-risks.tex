\section{Risks we found and how we treat them}\label{sec:risks}

Because every obligation is locked in full before it exists, no risk below can make a balance wrong or leave a
winner unpaid (G1--G3). What remains is risk to \emph{service}: how long money stays locked, whether a deal happens at
all, and who sees prices first. We rank by the worst effect we measured on locked capital and lost service. Every
rank-1 to rank-5 risk was found by a run that made it bind; most come from an interaction between two of our own
rules or between a rule and the transport.

\textbf{Interactions we found between rules.}
(a) \emph{Pull rule $\times$ endpoint timer.} The pull rule makes recovery safe, but it waits for $R_e$, so recovery
after an isolation lags reconnection by up to one $R_e$ (6.1 h in S2; up to 36 h on far pairs). Resubmitting as soon as
any packet from the peer arrives would cut this; it costs quota and is an \extension.
(b) \emph{Session pinning $\times$ known-closure waiting $\times$ 30-day lifetime.} The brief lets a sender wait
for a known closure, and the session is pinned; together they park a SETTLE behind a 51.8-day closure until it expires
(risk 2). R16 breaks the chain.
(c) \emph{Offer expiry $\times$ endpoint and hop timers} (risk 4): two individually sensible numbers, 24 h and
32.4 h, made the first retry useless, and our first fix (live $T_0 + R_e$) still failed
\EtwoVMEarthDeclineFortyEight\% of the time because it ignored hop retries.
(d) \emph{Reverse-link closure $\times$ hop receipt.} A packet launched just before its reverse link closes arrives,
but its receipt cannot come back, so the sender retries into a closed link for up to four $R_h$ (E4 boundary, Earth:
49.6 min window). R16 forbids such launches.
(e) \emph{Freeze $\times$ maintenance.} Our freeze starts at h 264, exactly when B--Ceres maintenance ends, so Jupiter
takers get the h 240 print only after the freeze has begun; we accept this because no money depends on it.
