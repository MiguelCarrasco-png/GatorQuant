\section*{S2. Stress and recovery}

\textbf{Incident chosen and why.} The referee of every price contract is Ceres, so we cut Ceres off at maturity: a 72 h isolation, h 286--358,
on the \emph{rising} run (FR), the harder one, because the winner, Callisto, is remote and waits for both its payoff and its pledge; in the falling
run (FF) the winner Ceres Iron Works is paid locally and only Callisto's refund waits. We scanned every kind, node and start time for a single
incident (\texttt{code/s2\_incident.py}; table below, each at its worst start) and then \emph{stacked} the three the brief allows
(\texttt{code/stack\_scan.py}): isolation of Ceres starting h 272.5, 280 or 286; an endpoint reset of Ceres or Jupiter at the reconnection, 2 h or 5 h
later; a 6 h forced loss of Jupiter's links at h 344 or 358 or none: \StackCombos\ combinations, each a full simulator run, chosen to hurt, not to
please. The worst resumes service at h \StackWorstH\ (median \StackMedianH). Retries cannot beat an isolation: endpoint attempts are $R_e = 25.4$ h apart.

\textbf{What the rules did.} The timer-only version of R8 (resubmit every $R_e$) resumed at h 364.83, 76.1 h late with 164 launches. R8 as now written
resubmits every $P = 2.4$ h in one batch per peer, on contact, and from a fresh session. Resubmitting on contact alone changes nothing in S2: both sides are silent
from h 358 until their timers fire. Pace is the lever: the pledge holder's 10-packet reserve affords one packet per 2.4 h.

\textbf{State of every obligation during S2-FR.} At the print (h 288.0003) the payoff \$57{,}500 moves from Ceres Iron Works' margin to Jupiter Branch's
account at Ceres, and its other \$17{,}500 is free: Ceres keeps working (local access is unaffected). At Jupiter, Callisto's \$75{,}000 stays locked: Jupiter Branch owes it back and
owes the \$57{,}500 payoff as a claim backed by its new holding at Ceres, \$132{,}500 in all, which Callisto cannot touch (R2) and Jupiter Branch will not
release on a timer (R6). Every obligation keeps a named, funded owner; the other eight settlements are untouched. \textbf{Financial service resumes at
h \SimSTwoFRSpendable}, \SimSTwoFRLost\ h after the no-incident run, at \SimSTwoFRExtraDollarHours\ extra locked \$-hours; later answers to copies
are duplicates and are ignored. In the stacked run service resumes at h \SimStackSpendable\ (\SimStackLost\ h late, \SimStackExtraDollarHours\ extra \$-hours).

\textbf{Faulty-Branch test} (R22 \extension; \texttt{code/faulty.py}). The brief assumes Branches follow the rules; four injected faults show what detection buys
and what it does not. Each fault is a record arriving at the lock holder; the rows show the run with R22 and without.
\begin{center}\gen{faulty_table.tex}\end{center}
The echo check and the recompute need no new bytes: the settling print's value rides in 4 of the record's 4 reserved bytes and identities are unforgeable. They detect a
lying referee or decider; they cannot detect silence, and nothing here enforces anything against a Branch that colludes with its own clients.

\textbf{Quota exhaustion} (R14, R18; \texttt{code/quota\_test.py}). Earth Branch's slice is cut from 66 to $S$ packets per 24 h, Neptune is isolated h 1--73, and Terra tries to
accept 40 offers at h 0 (40 locks need 3 batched packets). New locks may use $S-10$ packets (``reserve 10''); the ablation lets them use all $S$ (``reserve 0'').
\begin{center}\gen{quota_test.tex}\end{center}
New trade queues and is refused when its offer life runs out (12 of 40 at $S=11$); every admitted lock still ends and no window exceeds $S$. The ablation completes just as
fast: batching makes the reserve unnecessary at these loads, so we claim it as insurance, not as something the test proved needed.

\textbf{Griefing and a silent Price Board.} A seller who withdraws after the buyer's LOCK is sent freezes \$25{,}000 for \GriefVMHours\ h (\GriefVMDollarHours\ \$-hours;
\GriefUranusHours\ h from Uranus): the lock is never broken by a timer. R24 bounds repeat offenders. In run VOID no settling print exists; R23 voids the contract after
24 h and service resumes at h \SimVoidSpendable\ (\SimVoidExtraDollarHours\ extra \$-hours, nothing paid).

\textbf{Maintenance and the alternative design.} Removing both maintenance windows (geometry kept) changes no completion time, packet count or \$-hour figure in any
run: R15 pins around the B--Neptune window and the B--Ceres window only delays the information-only h 240 print. \textbf{One Mars hub ledger} holding every client's
account, with the same funding and guarantees: far clients finish sooner (Uranus 12.37 h $\to$ 8.23 h) but every trade takes two hub legs, twice the packets (56 vs 28)
and quota (6 vs 3), two sessions to pre-open, Ares locked twice as long, and one settlement whose isolation stops all cross-planet trade. We keep home ledgers.
A \emph{second} pre-opened Jupiter--Ceres session on Relay A cuts the forced-loss case from 25.4 h to 7.1 h and does nothing against isolation.
