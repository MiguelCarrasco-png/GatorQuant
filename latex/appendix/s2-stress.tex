\section*{S2. Stress and recovery}

\textbf{Incident chosen: a 72 h isolation of Ceres, h 286--358, on the falling run (FF).} Ceres is the referee and
the price source, so every price contract depends on it, and in FF the loser (Callisto) is remote, so its pledge and
refund can only be settled by a SETTLE that must leave Ceres. We scanned every kind, node and start time
(\texttt{code/s2\_incident.py}); the worst cases, each at its worst start:
\begin{center}\gen{s2_incident.tex}\end{center}
Retries cannot beat an isolation: an endpoint packet gets four attempts $R_e = 2T_0 + 24 = 25.4$ h apart, so the first
three fall inside 72 h and only the fourth gets out. Any start in [272.5, 288] gives the same 76.1 h; we chose
h 286 (inside the band, 2 h before the print) over the equally long isolation at opening because it hits a
\emph{decided} debt rather than a position not yet open. A 6 h forced loss swallows all four hop launches
($3R_h = 5.1$ h $< 6$ h) and costs one endpoint cycle; a reset costs 1--3 h, because durable records survive and the
re-handshake plus pull recover it.

\textbf{State of every obligation during the incident.} The position was discharged at h 288.0003 on the Ceres
ledger, which keeps working (local access is not affected). Ceres Iron Works holds its \$57{,}500 payoff as a backed
claim, usable at Ceres, and its own \$75{,}000 margin is free. At Jupiter, Callisto's \$75{,}000 stays locked: it
owes \$57{,}500 of it to Ceres Branch (a recorded pending release at Ceres) and is owed the other \$17{,}500 back. No
one can move the pledge: Callisto cannot unlock it (R2) and Jupiter Branch will not release it on a timer (R6).
Every obligation keeps a named, funded owner throughout. Other settlements are untouched.

\textbf{Lost service and recovery.} SETTLE's first three endpoint attempts (h 288, 313.4, 338.8) and Jupiter's pulls
(h 313.4, 338.8) all fail; 20 launches are lost in all. Attempt 4 leaves Ceres at h 364.14 and arrives at Jupiter at
h \SimSTwoSpendable: the pledge is debited \$57{,}500 to Ceres Branch's account and \$17{,}500 is spendable by
Callisto. \textbf{Financial service resumes at h \SimSTwoSpendable}, \SimSTwoLost\ h after the no-incident run, at a
cost of \SimSTwoExtraDollarHours\ extra locked \$-hours. Ceres's later answer to Jupiter's pull (arriving h 365.52) is
a duplicate and is ignored. S2 ends at h 365 with every obligation settled. The 6.1 h between reconnection (h 358)
and attempt 4 is risk 1's residual in the paper.

\textbf{Maintenance comparison.} Removing both maintenance windows (geometry kept) changes no completion time, no
packet count and no \$-hour figure. The B--Neptune window [2, 26) is avoided because R15 pins the value move via
Relay A; the B--Ceres window [240, 264) only delays the information-only h 240 print (the 24 h wait in the table).
Maintenance costs our design nothing because foresight pinning plans around it.

\textbf{Alternative design, same funding and guarantees: one Mars hub ledger.} A hub at Mars keeps every client's
account. Far clients finish sooner (Uranus 12.37 h $\to$ 8.23 h), but every trade takes two hub legs: twice the
packets (56 vs 28) and quota (6 vs 3), two sessions to pre-open, Ares locked twice as long (1{,}646 vs 835
share-hours), and one settlement whose isolation stops all cross-planet trade. We keep home ledgers. A cheaper
sensitivity check: a second pre-opened Jupiter--Ceres session on Relay A cuts the forced-loss case from 25.4 h to
7.1 h but does nothing against isolation, so it is not worth its quota.
