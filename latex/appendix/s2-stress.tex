\section*{S2. Stress and recovery}

\textbf{Incident chosen and why.} Ceres is the referee of every price contract, so we cut it off at maturity: a 72 h isolation, h 286--358, on the \emph{rising} run (FR), the
harder one, because the winner Callisto is remote and waits for payoff and pledge; in FF the winner is paid locally and only Callisto's refund waits. We scanned every
single incident (\texttt{code/s2\_incident.py}; table, each at its worst start) and then \emph{stacked} the three the brief allows (\texttt{code/stack\_scan.py}): isolation of
Ceres from h 272.5, 280 or 286; an endpoint reset of Ceres or Jupiter at reconnection, 0, 2 or 5 h later; a 6 h forced loss of Jupiter's links at h 344 or 358 or none. Of
\StackCombos\ full simulator runs the worst resumes service at h \StackWorstH\ (median \StackMedianH); we report that one, chosen to hurt.

$P = 2.4$ h in one batch per peer, on contact, and from a fresh session. Contact alone changes nothing in S2: both sides are silent from h 358 until their timers fire. Pace is the lever,
and the 10-packet reserve affords one packet per 2.4 h:

\textbf{State of every obligation in S2-FR.} At the print (h 288.0003) the payoff \$57{,}500 moves from Ceres Iron Works' margin to Jupiter Branch's account at Ceres and the other
\$17{,}500 is free; Ceres keeps working. At Jupiter, Callisto's \$75{,}000 stays locked: Jupiter Branch owes it back and owes the payoff as a claim backed by its new holding at Ceres,
\$132{,}500 in all, which Callisto cannot touch (R2) and Jupiter will not release on a timer (R6). Every obligation keeps a funded owner; the other eight settlements are untouched.

\textbf{Faulty-Branch test} (R22 \extension; \texttt{code/faulty.py}). The brief assumes Branches follow the rules; four faults, each a record arriving at the lock holder, show what
detection buys and what it does not.
\begin{center}\gen{faulty_table.tex}\end{center}
The echo check and recompute need no new bytes (the settling print's value rides in the SETTLE's reserved bytes; identities are unforgeable). They catch a lying referee or decider, not
silence, and nothing here stops a Branch that colludes with its own clients.

\textbf{Quota exhaustion} (R14, R18; \texttt{code/quota\_test.py}). Earth Branch's slice is cut from 66 to $S$, Neptune is isolated h 1--73, and Terra accepts 40 offers at h 0 (3 batched
packets). New locks may use $S-10$ packets (``reserve 10''); the ablation lets them use all $S$.
\begin{center}\gen{quota_test.tex}\end{center}
New trade queues and is refused when its offer life runs out (12 of 40 at $S=11$); every admitted lock still ends and no window exceeds $S$. The ablation finishes as fast, so the reserve is
insurance this test cannot show to be necessary.

\textbf{Griefing, silent Price Board, maintenance, hub.} A seller who withdraws after the LOCK is sent freezes \$25{,}000 for \GriefVMHours\ h (\GriefVMDollarHours\ \$-hours; \GriefUranusHours\ h
from Uranus); R24 bounds repeat offenders. In run VOID no settling print exists; R23 voids after 24 h and service resumes at h \SimVoidSpendable\ (\SimVoidExtraDollarHours\ extra
\$-hours, nothing paid). Removing both maintenance windows changes no completion time, packet count or \$-hour figure in any run. A \emph{Mars hub ledger} with the same funding finishes far clients sooner (Uranus 12.37 h $\to$ 8.23 h) but takes twice the packets (56 vs 28) and
quota, two pre-opened sessions, twice the Ares-hours, and one settlement whose isolation stops all cross-planet trade; we keep home ledgers.
