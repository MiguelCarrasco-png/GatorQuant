\section*{E1. Orbital calculations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{E1}

\textbf{Method} (\texttt{code/orbits.py}). Frame: heliocentric, geometric, J2000 ecliptic, positions in AU. Time:
hours $h$ since $t=0$ (2026-09-22 00:00 TDB); days $= h/24$. Each body follows its fixed Kepler ellipse from the
supplied elements: $M = M_0 + n\cdot\text{days} \bmod 360^\circ$; Kepler's equation $E - e\sin E = M$ by Newton's
method from $E_0 = M$, stopped when the step is below $10^{-14}$ rad; $x' = a(\cos E - e)$,
$y' = a\sqrt{1-e^2}\sin E$; rotation by $\omega$, then $i$, then $\Omega$. Relays: prograde circles of radius
$\sqrt8$ AU in the reference plane, phases $45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$, period $365.2568983 \times 8^{1.5}$ d
$= 1{,}737.46$ d. Times before 0 use the same formulas backward.

\textbf{Light time.} For a launch at $t_e$ from sender $S$ to receiver $R$ we solve
$t_a = t_e + 8.317\,\text{min/AU} \times |r_R(t_a) - r_S(t_e)|$ by fixed-point iteration from $t_a^{(0)}$ = the
frozen-receiver value, stopping when an update is below 1 ms. The map contracts by $|v_R|/c \le 2\times10^{-4}$, so
three iterates always suffice. The photon path is the segment $r_S(t_e) \to r_R(t_a)$; it is blocked if its closest
approach to the Sun is below 0.10 AU (closest point by projection, clipped to the segment). A launch is ready 1 s
after the packet is (serialization), a relay adds 1 s, and local access adds 1 s at each end of client traffic only.

\textbf{Epoch check, periods and radius bounds.} The worst epoch error is \EoneWorstErr\ AU, against the brief's
$10^{-5}$. After one period every body returns to its start within \EoneWorstClose\ AU, and the distance from the
Sun over a period (20{,}001 samples) stays inside $[a(1-e), a(1+e)]$ to \EoneRadSlack\ AU.

\textbf{Receiver motion changes the arrival time; forward and return differ.} We scanned every ordered pair of the
11 nodes, hourly over h 0--2{,}000, for the largest gap between the moving-receiver arrival and the frozen-receiver
value. It is \EoneExSender\ $\to$ \EoneExReceiver\ at h \EoneExTe\ (a direct path, used here only as the worked
example). Frozen receiver: \EoneFrozenMin\ min. Iterates: \EoneItA, \EoneItB, \EoneItC\ min (converged in
\EoneFwdIters\ evaluations). \EoneExReceiver\ moves at \EoneRxRadialKms\ km/s along the line of sight during the
4.1 h flight, so the photon arrives \EoneFwdShiftS\ s later than the frozen value. The \emph{return} message, emitted
at the same instant, takes \EoneRevMin\ min (\EoneRevShiftS\ s; \EoneExSender\ barely moves). Forward and return
therefore differ by \EoneAsymS\ s, 2{,}900 times the 1 ms tolerance and more than the 1 s serialization step: it
matters for the brief's simultaneous-arrival ordering and for whether a flight overlaps a maintenance window. Every
hop of our scenario routes, each direction computed separately at its own launch time:
