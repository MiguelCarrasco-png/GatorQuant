\section*{E1. Orbital calculations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{E1}

\textbf{Method} (\texttt{code/orbits.py}). Frame: heliocentric, geometric, J2000 ecliptic, AU. Time: hours $h$ since $t=0$ (2026-09-22 00:00 TDB); negative times use the same
formulas. Each body follows its fixed Kepler ellipse: $M = M_0 + n\cdot\text{days} \bmod 360^\circ$; $E - e\sin E = M$ by Newton's method from $E_0 = M$ until the step is below
$10^{-14}$ rad; $x' = a(\cos E - e)$, $y' = a\sqrt{1-e^2}\sin E$; rotation by $\omega$, $i$, $\Omega$. Relays: prograde circles of radius $\sqrt8$ AU in the reference plane, phases
$45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$, period $365.2568983 \times 8^{1.5}$ d $= 1{,}737.46$ d.

\textbf{Light time.} For a launch at $t_e$ from $S$ to $R$ we solve $t_a = t_e + 8.317\,\text{min/AU} \times |r_R(t_a) - r_S(t_e)|$ by fixed-point iteration from the frozen-receiver
value until an update is below 1 ms; the map contracts by $|v_R|/c \le 2\times10^{-4}$, so three iterates suffice. The path is blocked if its closest approach to the Sun (projection
clipped to the segment) is below 0.10 AU. Serialization adds 1 s per launch, a relay 1 s, and local access 1 s at each end of client traffic only.

\textbf{Epoch check, periods and radius bounds.} The worst epoch error is \EoneWorstErr\ AU (brief: $10^{-5}$). After one period every body returns within \EoneWorstClose\ AU, and its
distance from the Sun (20{,}001 samples) stays inside $[a(1-e), a(1+e)]$ to \EoneRadSlack\ AU.
