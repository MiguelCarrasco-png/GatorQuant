\section*{E5. Shifted epochs (Tier 3 claimed)}

Each run starts at $t = k \times 365.25$ d with planets and relays advanced and balances reset to the opening sheet;
maintenance and the S2 incident are not replayed; prints and maturity shift with the run (\texttt{code/e5\_epochs.py},
the S1 scenario code); sessions open 72 h ahead on the foresight route.
\textbf{Tier 1.} At +1, +10 and +100 years both scenarios complete with no suspension. Delays move with geometry
(value move 8.65--9.61 h; the future settles 0.76--1.46 h after its print); +100 y pins via Relay B.

\textbf{Tier 2: the difficult epoch.} Of the 54 closures of a Jupiter or Ceres relay link in E4's 200 years, we placed
a run so that one hits the future's pinned route between the last interim print and the settling print, making SETTLE
the first message to meet it. The one closed longest after maturity starts at h \EfiveHardH\ (+\EfiveHardY\ y;
\EfiveHardDate): Jupiter--Relay A closes at run hour \EfiveClosureStart, 6 h before the print, for
\EfiveClosureDays\ days (\EfiveClosureH\ h, beyond the 30-day packet lifetime). \textbf{The rising run is harder:} the
long, Callisto at Jupiter, is paid only when SETTLE crosses the closed link; in the falling run only its refund waits.

\textbf{Tier 3: both directions.} R16 sees the closure before SETTLE is sent (h \EfiveRerouteAt), opens a fresh
session via Relay B, and SETTLE is spendable at h \EfiveFRDone, \EfiveLate\ h after the open-route time, in both
runs. Queuing behind the closure, as the brief's waiting rule alone would, holds SETTLE \EfiveNaiveLate\ h with 514
launches. \textbf{Funded consequences:} nothing is suspended; the cost is \EfiveExtraUsdH\ extra locked \$-hours and
one quota packet. Mirrored paths, margin rule and route give the two runs identical times and packet counts.
