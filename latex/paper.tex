\begin{abstract}
We run the exchange as nine \emph{Branches}, one per settlement, each keeping the only ledger for the assets held
there; there is no hub and no central bank. Nothing is owed across light-lag unless it is locked first: a cross-planet
trade locks the buyer's money at home, the seller's Branch decides once and commits, and the buyer's Branch releases
on the commit; a capped Ceres Iron future locks each side's full maximum loss at home and is settled by the Branch
where its price is published. No timer ends a lock and only the lock holder resends, so a lost, late, duplicated or
contradictory message costs \emph{time}, not principal, and time has a price: the 288-hour future keeps 30\% of all
cash locked, \SimFutDollarHours\ \$-hours, and each incident adds more (below). The invariants (G1--G3) rest on rules
alone and are checked after every ledger step of \FuzzRuns\ fault-injected runs, \FuzzFailures\ violations. Completion needs only that two Branches can eventually exchange a packet, which a 200-year scan certified to 1 ms
shows is always possible on the common model. On the traced runs an Earth--Neptune share trade
completes in \SimVMComplete\ h and the future pays in full in both price directions at h \SimFutSpendable. A 72 h
isolation of the referee at maturity makes the remote winner (FR: Callisto) wait \SimSTwoFRLost\ h more
(\SimSTwoFRExtraDollarHours\ extra \$-hours); our worst stacked incident (isolation, reset, forced loss) makes it wait
\SimStackLost\ h (\SimStackExtraDollarHours). No balance is wrong at any point. Guarantees against a Branch that breaks
its own rules are an \extension: detection is implemented and tested (R22), enforcement is not claimed.
\end{abstract}
